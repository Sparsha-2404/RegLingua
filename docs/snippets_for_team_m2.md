## A. For M4 - README and demo script

### A1. Model description (paste into the README "Risk classifier" section)
Risk classification is done by M2's baseline classifier: TF-IDF features (word 1-2-grams) with a class-balanced logistic regression,
trained on 412 of M1's labelled statements (SEBI, RBI and DPDP topics; English, Hindi and code-mixed Hinglish) and tested on
104 held-out statements. For every input it returns a risk level (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW, shown as Risky / Ambiguous / Compliant) and
a confidence score, which is the model's probability for the level it chose. It runs fully offline and is deterministic. It is a research
prototype for decision support, not a legal certification or compliance guarantee.

### A2. How confidence and abstention appear in the UI
- **Confidence** is the predicted level's probability. It is a model score, not a calibrated chance of being right: on held-out rows its
  expected calibration error is 0.141 (95% CI 0.074-0.241) (top-label ECE, 10 bins). Label it "model confidence", not "probability of compliance".
- **Current Supervisor thresholds** (`agents/supervisor.py`, read-only): below 0.350 the case is not acted on
  (needs review); a Compliant label needs at least 0.700 to take the compliant fast path.
- **Day 9 operating points on the 104 held-out rows** (`data/evaluation/abstention_operating_points_m2.csv`; thresholds were
  chosen on non-held-out rows only):

| point | abstain floor | compliant floor | coverage (auto-accepted) | accuracy on accepted | false-compliant accepted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| balanced | 0.430 | 0.540 | 53.8% (95% CI 44.2-63.5%) | 71.4% (95% CI 58.8-83.0%) | 2 |
| permissive | 0.430 | 0.490 | 63.5% (95% CI 53.8-72.1%) | 63.6% (95% CI 51.4-75.0%) | 10 |
| conservative (compliant fast path off) | 0.460 | 1.000 | 38.5% (95% CI 29.8-47.1%) | 75.0% (95% CI 61.1-88.4%) | 0 |

  M2's finding, quoted from `data/evaluation/abstention_thresholds_m2.json`: "No operating point can safely auto-accept Compliant predictions. On non-held-out rows the most LOW predictions that can be accepted with zero false-compliant is 4 (exact 95% upper bound on the false-compliant rate 52.7%); the safety rule (upper bound <= 10%) needs at least 29 such acceptances with zero errors. The conservative point therefore switches compliant acceptance off (compliant_floor = 1.0)."
- **Wording for the UI:** show "Needs human review" for review/abstain routes; never present "Compliant" as a guarantee; show confidence as a
  score with the disclaimer above. Display the evidence citations next to every label.

### A3. If asked "how accurate is it?"
"On 104 statements the model had not seen in training, accuracy was 61.5% (95% CI 51.9-71.0%) and macro-F1 was 0.640 (95% CI 0.541-0.729).
Two cautions. First, that split was not grouped by statement: 76 of the 93 held-out statements have their
English or Hindi translation in the training data, so it can overstate accuracy. A cross-validation grouped by original statement on the
412 training rows, which keeps translations together, gives accuracy 60.7% (95% CI 55.6-65.8%) and macro-F1 0.626 (95% CI 0.571-0.677); the two
sets of figures are close and their intervals overlap. Second, the held-out set has only
43 English, 55 Hindi and 6 code-mixed statements (held-out accuracy 69.8% (95% CI 55.8-83.7%),
56.4% (95% CI 43.6-69.1%) and 50.0% (95% CI 16.7-83.3%) respectively), so the per-language figures, and especially the code-mixed one, are too
small to rely on. It has not been tested on real-world marketing text from outside this dataset, and it is decision support, not a legal opinion."

(Held-out predictions come from the committed model (`prob_source` = "committed" in `confidence_signals_m2.csv`); the cross-validated figures
use grouped out-of-fold predictions (`prob_source` = "oof_fold"). A frozen final test split with a grouped protocol is pending in this checkout,
so these are the best figures available today.)

## B. For M3 - agent documentation (retriever)

### B1. Evaluation results (source-level retrieval of the correct regulatory document)
Retrieval was evaluated on 510 queries with the true regulator supplied as a filter ("oracle" filter).

| retriever | Recall@1 | Recall@3 | Recall@5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| keyword only | 50.6% (95% CI 46.3-54.9%) | 62.0% (95% CI 57.6-66.1%) | 67.3% (95% CI 63.1-71.2%) |
| hybrid (keyword + embeddings) | 67.3% (95% CI 63.1-71.4%) | 99.6% (95% CI 99.0-100.0%) | 100.0% (95% CI 100.0-100.0%) |

Hybrid Recall@1 by language: English 68.4% (95% CI 62.0-73.9%) (n = 237), Hindi 63.7% (95% CI 57.4-70.0%)
(n = 237), code-mixed 83.3% (95% CI 69.4-94.4%) (n = 36). Keyword-only Hindi Recall@1 is
27.4% (95% CI 21.9-32.9%) (Hindi discussion: `docs/day5_retrieval_evaluation_m2.md`).

End-to-end with the *predicted* regulator (Day 6 pipeline, expected source available for 510 rows): the correct source is ranked first for
66.3% and within the top 3 for 98.8% of rows (69.2% and 96.2% on the
104 held-out rows). Predicted-regulator accuracy on held-out rows is 93.3%.

### B2. Known limits (paste next to the results)
- Table B1 uses an oracle regulator filter, so Recall@3 and Recall@5 are close to ceiling by construction; Recall@1 is the informative number, and
  67.3% means roughly one in three queries still ranks a wrong source first (`docs/day5_retrieval_evaluation_m2.md`).
- Hits are counted at source (document) level, not passage level; the EvidenceAgent needs the right passage, so chunk-level evidence selection is
  not yet measured.
- The code-mixed group is small (n = 36); do not read its figure as an advantage or a weakness.
- Integration check (read-only, `python scripts\check_demo_readiness.py`, row R1): in this checkout `agents/agent_factory.py` still injects the
  keyword-only `DeterministicRetriever`, so the running demo does not use the hybrid retriever measured above until it is wired in (M3/M4 decision).
- The hybrid results file was committed in 1b3a167, after the HybridRetriever fix d72dfb2 in git history; rerun `python scripts\evaluate_retrieval_recall_precision.py --hybrid`
  (bash: `python scripts/evaluate_retrieval_recall_precision.py --hybrid`) after any retriever change.
